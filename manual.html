The Official Playbook User Manual

A comprehensive guide to utilizing Playbook's Playbook grading engine effectively and securely.

System Overview

Playbook is a high-end, AI-powered grading SaaS tailored specifically for educators. It is designed with a "local-first" architecture, meaning the application runs entirely within your browser for maximum speed and data privacy. It leverages state-of-the-art vision models to evaluate handwritten and typed exams against your custom marking schemes.

Core Capabilities (What We Do)

  • Bulk PDF Scanning & Separation: Upload a single PDF containing an entire class's exams. The system uses a "Blank Page" heuristic—it analyzes the pixels of each page, and whenever it detects a blank page, it separates the preceding pages into an individual student's exam.
  • Playbook Vision Engine: Playbook integrates with the OpenRouter API. It prompts advanced vision models (like gpt-4o) to act as a strict, highly experienced University Professor. It evaluates all pages of a student's submission simultaneously.
  • Intelligent Grading: The Playbook awards partial marks fairly where steps are correct, refuses to give free marks, and avoids unfair deductions based on the precise marking scheme you provide.
  • "Human-in-the-Loop" Override: The Playbook is a tool, not a replacement. You can review every score and piece of feedback generated by the AI. If you disagree, you can instantly override the score or edit the feedback before finalizing.
  • Local Database & Analytics: All data is stored in IndexedDB within your browser. Playbook generates score distributions, identifies struggling concepts, and prepares individual feedback sheets for download.

Strict Limitations (What We DON'T Do)

⚠️ Data Syncing & Cloud Storage: All data lives exclusively in your browser's local IndexedDB. We do not currently have a cloud backend. If you clear your browser's site data, use incognito mode, or switch to a different computer, your sessions, configurations, and grading data will be permanently lost.

⚠️ API Key Management: Playbook does not provide Playbook access out of the box. You must provide your own valid OpenRouter API Key (with vision-capable models enabled and funded) for the grading engine to function.

⚠️ Playbook Hallucinations & Vision Errors: Playbook vision models are not perfect. Extremely messy handwriting, poor scan quality, or ambiguous answers might be misread. This reinforces the absolute necessity of the "Human-in-the-Loop" override step.

The Playbook Standard Marking Scheme Format

To guarantee 100% deterministic grading across thousands of exams (including diagrams, codes, and complex calculations), you must format your Marking Scheme text file according to the strict rules below. Do not explain the Playbook to the system; simply provide explicit, mechanical instructions.

1. Hierarchical Question Separation

Clearly separate questions and their sub-parts using a consistent alphanumeric hierarchy. Always define the maximum score for the specific sub-part.

  • Use standard formats like Question 1, 1a, 1b(i).
  • Do not group answers. Each sub-part must have its own dedicated grading criteria.

2. Explicit Point Allocation

State exactly how many marks are awarded for each specific keyword, step, or concept. Leave zero ambiguity for the AI.

  • Use phrases like: "Award [1 mark] for mentioning 'Chlorophyll'."
  • For math/code: "Award [1 mark] for the correct formula. Award [1 mark] for the correct final answer (42)."

3. Strict Rules for Visuals, Diagrams & Code

When asking students to draw, sketch, or code, you must define the exact structural elements the Playbook should look for to prevent hallucination or cheating.

  • Diagrams: Define the required shapes and explicit labels. Example: "Award [1 mark] if a circle representing the nucleus is drawn. Award [1 mark] if the line pointing to the circle is explicitly labeled 'Nucleus'."
  • Code: Specify required syntax or logic. Example: "Award [2 marks] if a 'for' loop is used. Deduct [1 mark] if a semicolon is missing at the end of line 3."

4. Before & After Example

❌ Bad Format (Ambiguous)

Question 1 (5 marks)
The student should explain photosynthesis. They need to mention the sun, water, and chlorophyll. If they draw a good diagram, give them full marks. Otherwise give them partial credit.

✅ Playbook Standard Format

Question 1: Photosynthesis (Total: 5 marks)

1a: Definition (Max: 3 marks)
- Award [1 mark] for stating "conversion of light energy to chemical energy".
- Award [1 mark] for explicitly writing the word "Chlorophyll".
- Award [1 mark] for mentioning "Water" or "H2O".

1b: Diagram (Max: 2 marks)
- Award [1 mark] if a leaf shape is drawn.
- Award [1 mark] ONLY IF an arrow is drawn pointing into the leaf and is explicitly labeled "Sunlight".

Step-by-Step Workflow (How to Use It)

  1. Step 1: Setting up the API Key. Navigate to the Dashboard. In the "API Configuration" section, paste your OpenRouter API Key and click "Save Key".
  2. Step 2: Preparing Physical Exams. Collect all student exams. Crucially: Insert exactly one blank sheet of paper between Student A's exam and Student B's exam. Scan the entire stack into a single, high-quality PDF file.
  3. Step 3: Uploading & Initiating. Go to the "Upload Exams" tab. Drag and drop your marking scheme (as a .txt file) and your bulk PDF. Enter a Session Name and click "Start Playbook Grading". Wait while the system processes the images and queries the AI.
  4. Step 4: Reviewing & Overriding. You will be redirected to the Review interface. Navigate through each student's exam. Read the AI's analysis and suggested feedback. If adjustments are needed, click "Override", change the score/text, and click "Save".
  5. Step 5: Finalizing & Exporting. Once all students are reviewed, click "Finalize Scores". In the Analytics dashboard, you can view class trends, print reports, download a CSV of all grades, and download detailed individual feedback text files for your students.

Troubleshooting & Data Safety

Playbook Fails to Grade a Page: If you receive an API error or a timeout during the upload phase, check your OpenRouter API balance and ensure your internet connection is stable. If a specific student's exam fails, the system will generate a fallback score of 0 with an error message in the feedback box. You can manually grade this student using the Override feature.

Separation Errors: If the system fails to separate students correctly, ensure that the blank pages you scanned are completely free of stray marks, lines, or significant scanner dust, as the heuristic relies on pixel brightness.

Data Safety: Because data is stored locally, always download your CSV results and feedback sheets immediately after finalizing a session. Do not rely on the browser to store historical records long-term.
